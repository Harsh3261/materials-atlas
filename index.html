Equivalents — near is not identical

42CrMo (GB/T 3077) vs AISI 4140: NEAR — NOT INTERCHANGEABLE same Cr-Mo idea, different windows — 42CrMo Mn is 0.50–0.80%, 4140 Mn is 0.75–1.00%. A heat can pass one and fail the other on manganese alone. And the mechanical bases differ entirely: GB/T 3077 publishes Q&T reference-bar minimums; SAE J404 publishes no mechanicals at all.

40Cr vs AISI 5140: 40Cr's Cr window is 0.80–1.10% vs 5140's 0.70–0.90% — a 40Cr heat at 1.05% Cr is out of 5140 spec.

304 (ASTM A240) vs 1.4301 (EN 10088): NEAR — NOT INTERCHANGEABLE the canonical pair, but NEAR under the strict rule — the windows cross rather than nest: carbon is 0.07 max in both (the 0.08 figure sometimes quoted for A240 is the A276 bar limit, not A240), and the two standards set their remaining limits and mechanical minimums differently, so neither window contains the other. The EN minimum Rp0.2 (230 MPa, cold-rolled strip) is higher than A240's 205 MPa. Dual-certified material exists precisely because neither standard contains the other.

E355 / ST52.3 / “1026-class” honed tube: three different documents (EN 10305-1, withdrawn DIN 2391, ASTM A519) with three different property bases — spec minimums by delivery condition, legacy minimums, and typicals. Same barrel application, not the same guarantee.

20CrMo's EN counterpart is genuinely disputed — crosswalks list both 18CrMo4 (1.7243) and 25CrMo4 (1.7218), and neither reaches two-source confirmation, so the atlas asserts no EN designation for it (its AISI cross is likewise disputed). JIS SCM420 is the corroborated cross. C36000 vs CW603N is the standard free-cutting-brass pair, but Pb windows and temper designations differ — a cert must name the system it certifies to.

4340 (SAE J404) vs EN 34CrNiMo6 / 1.6582: XREF-ONLY Conventional cross-reference only — EN ISO 683-2 34CrNiMo6 (C 0.30–0.38, Cr 1.30–1.70, Ni 1.30–1.70) vs SAE J404 4340 (C 0.38–0.43, Cr 0.70–0.90, Ni 1.65–2.00): the Cr and Ni bands are disjoint. The JIS SNCM439 entry stays NEAR. XREF-ONLY — a conventional cross-reference whose composition bands do not even overlap; never substitute on it.

C95400 (ASTM B505) vs EN CC331G / CuAl10Fe2-C: NEAR — NOT INTERCHANGEABLE CC333G (Ni 4–6) is disjoint from C95400 (Ni max 1.5) and pairs with C95500; C95400's EN casting neighbour is CC331G, still NEAR — its Al/Fe windows run lower.

Substitution rule: a near-equivalent may be proposed by a supplier; it is qualified only when chemistry (all restricted elements), condition, form/thickness band, and mechanical basis are each checked against the specified standard — or the substitution is formally approved as a deviation.

Check a cert — Step 1 · Chemistry

One flow, three steps: Step 1 — enter the chemistry here and get a verdict per element (never one verdict for the whole cert). Step 2 — the carbon equivalent below is computed from these same values. Step 3 — the qualify checklist finishes the cert. Two entry modes: verify against one grade, or identify from chemistry.

Type the chemistry off a mill cert — or paste it, or load a cert text/CSV file — and each element is checked against the grade's standard range. Blank fields are skipped; a value with no standard limit is reported as not checked, never passed silently.

Product analysis gets extra permitted variation beyond the heat-analysis ranges, set by the governing general-requirements standard (e.g. SAE J409 for J403/J404 grades, ASTM A751/A29 practice). Those tolerance tables were not verified into this atlas — so a product-analysis value just outside a range below is flagged, but confirm the permitted variation in the licensed standard before rejecting.

Type the values from a chemical analysis (wt% as reported). Blank = not entered. Every grade in the atlas's verified chemistry dataset is tested against the values you enter — an entered element a grade does not limit cannot exclude it, and is disclosed as not tested.

Step 2 · Carbon equivalent — computed from Step 1

CE (IIW) = C + Mn/6 + (Cr+Mo+V)/5 + (Ni+Cu)/15

No re-typing: this reads the chemistry entered in Step 1 (Verify mode) and updates as you type. CE and Pcm are screening guidance from standard welding practice (EN 1011 / AWS) — not a specification and not a pass/fail gate. Bands for CE: ≤ 0.40 generally weldable without special measures · 0.40–0.60 preheat likely · > 0.60 high hardenability — procedure-qualification territory. The IIW and Ito–Bessyo (Pcm) formulae measure different things and are not interchangeable.

Heat treatment — the condition is the material

Anneal — soften, improve machinability, relieve stress; the normal supply state for 4130/4140 bar before machining. Normalize — grain refinement and uniform properties (compare the A519 normalized rows against cold-worked). Quench — form martensite; the medium matters (the sourced rows show 4130 water-quenched, 4140/4340 oil-quenched). Temper — trade hardness for toughness. Carburize — hard case, tough core: 8620 and 20CrMo core properties are not case properties. Age — precipitation hardening: 17-4 PH H900, aluminium T6.

Hotter temper → softer, tougher. All three Q&T grades in the atlas (4130, 4140, 4340) show the same trend in the same source table. That is why a Q&T row without a tempering temperature is not a condition at all.

Flame-cut plate — the edge is no longer the grade you bought

Oxy-fuel cutting leaves a heat-affected zone: a thin hardened, sometimes micro-cracked edge layer on hardenable grades — and a softened band in Q&T plate (A514 class), where the cut heat over-tempers the edge. Higher carbon equivalent → deeper HAZ hardening and crack risk; thicker plate self-quenches the cut edge faster → harder HAZ. Check the PO/drawing for edge-condition requirements (grind or machine back the HAZ for fatigue or weld prep), and never certify a flame-cut edge's hardness as the plate's hardness.

The E355 lesson

EN 10305-1 delivery condition changes everything: +N (normalized) guarantees ReH ≥ 355 MPa, but +SR (stress relieved — the workhorse honed-barrel condition) guarantees ≥ 450 MPa. Online datasheets almost always quote the +N number and understate the steel by ~25%. Always match the cert's delivery condition to the row before comparing.

Conditions glossary — the condition word is half the spec

Sixteen condition terms that land on certs and POs — what each one means, and the check it demands.

The discriminator that matters on the floor: 4140 annealed or as-rolled reads ≈ HB 187–197; 4140 HTSR reads HB 311–331 typical. No overlap. A hardness reading near HB 300+ can never be as-rolled 4140, whatever the paperwork says — that single check would have caught an HRR bin holding HTSR stock.

Which standard can actually give you a number?

The most useful qualification skill is knowing which documents cannot. SAE J403/J404 set chemistry only — no strength values exist in them. ASTM A519's mechanical table is headed “typical.” A GB/T 3077 mechanical value exists only for the standard's own reference-bar heat treatment.

Data status key: SPEC MIN = guaranteed minimum (or specified range) from the governing standard's published table · TYPICAL = published average, not a guarantee, never for acceptance · MANUFACTURER = the maker's own published data. Bar-steel typicals carry their publisher's own warning: mill-lab averages on 1-in bars, a guide only — not a basis for acceptance or rejection. Section size matters throughout: GB/T 3077 minimums are 25 mm reference-bar values (15 mm for 20Mn2); Q&T properties fall off in heavy sections.
